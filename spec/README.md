# spec/: the grammars and the tables of the encoding

The pdp64 has one CPU: 32 registers (`r0`-`r31`) and a prefix-coded instruction encoding (the author, 2026-11-10, errata 122). It is exclusively a 32-register machine: two earlier iterations of the design, an encoding with sixteen registers in which parcel 0 is `len:2 opc:6 reg:4 low:4` (pdp64/r20) and a prefix-coded one with sixteen registers (pdp64/16), were built and measured against it, superseded and removed, and no tool has an option that selects another machine. Their grammars, tools and tests are in the git tags `pre-single-cpu` and `pre-code-removal`, and the measured comparison is in `docs/encoding-work/`.

The opcodes, prefixes and field positions may still change once the open items are decided and the encoding optimiser is run again; the grammars are then updated, and everything generated from them (the assembler tables, the tables of chapter 9 of the manual, the headers of the compiler) is regenerated. No document states these numbers in prose: they point to the generated tables.

| path | what it is |
|---|---|
| `grammars.d/` | the grammar, as a directory of numbered PARTS (errata 180): see "The drop-in directory" below. Every number, word, register, rule of the syntax and form of the encoding is in a part |
| `pdp64/pdp64.hbnf` | the master of the encoding (hand edited, directives only): `prefix none`, the include of `generated/index-enc.hbnf`, `%start insn`; hbnf reads the encoding through it |
| `pdp64-asm.hbnf` | the master of the assembly language (hand edited, directives only): `language C`, `whitespace ws`, the include of `generated/index-asm.hbnf`, `%start asm`; hbnf reads the assembler's grammar through it, as64 is built from it |
| `generated/index-enc.hbnf`, `index-asm.hbnf` | GENERATED by `tools/grammars_d.py`: the `include` lists of the parts of each target, in load order |
| `elf.hbnf` | the records of the ELF object file (a grammar of its own, read with `--root`; not a part) |
| `pdp64/tables.md` | the leaves, for people (`tools/mkenc.py --md`) |

The object files and executables name no instruction set: there is one, and the `e_flags` of an executable are 0 (`as64/relocs.h`); `ld64` and `sim64` refuse a file with an undefined `e_flags` bit.

The design, its measurements and the reasons are in `docs/encoding-proposal.md`; the scripts that produced the tables are in `docs/codegen-data/encoding/`.

## The grammars of the new encodings

The principle of the project is that the toolchain is written from one source of truth, a set of hbnf grammars, so that the assembler, the linker, the emulator, the
compiler back end and the QEMU target cannot drift apart. the encoding follows it: it is an hbnf grammar,

    spec/grammars.d/                 the parts: 10-enc-insn (`insn`), 20-enc-forms, 21-enc-shapes (the forms, leaves, shapes: generated), 22-enc-vocabulary (the header that defines every name),
                                     23-enc-registers (the name, the registers, the hot tables), 80-mn-ext (group mn-ext, generated)
    spec/pdp64/pdp64.hbnf            the master: directives and the include of the index of the parts that name `enc`

(the parts that say GENERATED are written by a tool; see "The drop-in directory" below)

An instruction is a bit record read from the top bit of parcel 0 (each parcel big-endian). A **leaf** is one alternative of `insn`: the prefix as a
constant (`0b0010`: 1 to 16 bits, as wide as its digits), the sub-opcode `sub:J`, and the pieces of the operands, `o2-r:4 o1-d-1:12 o1-d-2:16`, in the order of the bits; a field that
the layout splits is several pieces (`-1`, `-2`, from the top). A number of the sub-opcode that has no mnemonic is illegal (`?(sub < 30)`), a first parcel that no prefix matches is illegal.
A **form** (`form-LDU-B28 = o1:b4d28s-x1 o2:reg4`) lists the operand **shapes** of its leaves; a shape (`reg4 = r:4`, `b4d28s-x1 = base:4 d:28s`) is the operand as the assembly language
writes it, with the widths and signedness of its parts; its name carries what fields cannot say (the scale or unit of a displacement or an immediate, `sz` for "the size of the access").
The mnemonics of a leaf are the enum `sub-FORM-K = "movq" | "movl" | ...`, numbered by position. `forms = "SYS0"@0 | "SYSR" | ...` numbers the forms (the order in which the assembler
tries them), `hot3`, `hot4` are the hot-register tables, `regs` the register file, `fused` the fused instructions. The header of `grammars.d/22-enc-vocabulary.hbnf` is the reference for all names.

Everything is generated from these files; nothing is written by hand twice:

    spec/pdp64/pdp64.hbnf (with the files it includes)
        --hbnf-->        parse_bits / emit_bits   the decoder of the encoding as hbnf writes it (tests/enc/hbdec.c checks the codec against it, see below)
        --tools/mkenc.py (reading the grammar through tools/encgram.py)-->
                         build/pdp64-tab.c         the C tables of the codec (common/pdp64enc.c): forms, leaves, bit runs, mnemonics
                         build/pdp64-hbfields.h    the accessor that reads the raw fields out of hbnf's record (for the check)
                         build/x/*                 the assembly grammar of as64 (spec/ grammar + the new words + the registers r16-r31), the name tables
                         spec/pdp64/tables.md      the leaves, for people (checked by `mkenc.py --check`)

What hbnf itself generates, and what it cannot. hbnf (the commit in `tools/build-hbnf.sh`, `dd34982` when this was written, with no local patch) reads these grammars as they are: bit fields of any width,
constants as prefixes, `name:N` widths, signed `s`, enum numbers, `?(expr)` checks, `bits(0)` for an operand with no bits, alternatives of 270 leaves. It generates from them a complete
decoder and encoder (`parse_bits`, `emit_bits`), which is what `tests/enc/hbdec.c` compares with the codec. What no hbnf template produces is the *table* the codec runs on (leaves, runs of
bits, the candidate forms of each mnemonic, in the order the assembler tries them): that is a small program, `tools/mkenc.py`, which reads the grammar (`tools/encgram.py`: the subset of
hbnf the grammars use, with every consistency check it can make: each leaf covers its 16n bits once, each field has the width its shape says, the mnemonic counts fit the sub-opcode bits,
the catalogue is dense, `insn` lists every leaf) and nothing else. No data file stands beside the grammar. hbnf has two gaps that the grammars work around (neither blocks anything; see
`docs/errata.md`, entry 89, for the upstream proposal): a rule name cannot hold a dot or slash, so the optimiser's form names (`LDI12/a`) are kept as literals of the catalogue and the rule
names are their spelling with `-`; and a grammar that mixes bit rules with string-enum rules is read as bits only if its first rule is a bit rule (`insn` is the first rule), and a bit rule
needs a field to count as one (`insn = leaf:( ... )`).

## The drop-in directory (errata 179, 180)

The owner's words (2026-11-18): "for this time, we can break the byte identical requirement. shit just has to work. just get it done in a way where new grammars can be dropped in for individual parts, perhaps something like the dropdir formalization, grammars.d/NN-gram1.hbnf etc." and his standing rule: "any generated hbnf should be an include into the master hbnf file that is hand edited." His refinement: "and could even be grammars.d/NN-name/{prologue,grammar,epilogue}.hbnf so they can be assembled in their proper order." The future direction (his words): "later on a proper hbnf grammar would parse each hbnf file directly then do the shuffle so that prologues, grammars, and epilogues all get merged properly in the right sections of the 'one big grammar'".

**The grammar is a directory.** `spec/grammars.d/` holds the PARTS. A new part (a new instruction family, system registers, a hypervisor extension, crypto words, the next vector family) is a new file there: no other file is edited and no tool changes.

### The contract (what a native hbnf loader would have to honour; the script `tools/grammars_d.py` is a text-level stopgap for it)

1. **Layout.** A part is a file `grammars.d/NN-name.hbnf` (one section, `grammar`) or a directory `grammars.d/NN-name/` holding any of `prologue.hbnf`, `grammar.hbnf`, `epilogue.hbnf` (at least one; no other `.hbnf`). `NN` is two digits, `name` is lowercase letters, digits and hyphens. A file or directory that does not follow the pattern is an error (a `.hbnf` that is not numbered; a README or `99-x.hbnf.disabled` is not read: it does not end in `.hbnf`). A number is used once (`NN`), across files and directories.
2. **Order: three passes.** The grammar is the concatenation, in this order, of every part's `prologue`, then every part's `grammar`, then every part's `epilogue`; inside a pass, the order of `NN`. The prologue holds what must come before any rule (the rule that must be first, `insn`; hbnf directives); the grammar holds the rules; the epilogue holds what must come last (a rule that collects what the earlier parts defined, such as `mnemonic`; extensions with `=/`). A directive (`include`, `prefix`, `language`, `whitespace`, `%start`, ...) is allowed in a prologue only.
3. **Targets.** Each section file says in its first comment lines `; targets: asm enc spec`: the grammars it belongs to. `asm` is the assembly language (hbnf reads it through `spec/pdp64-asm.hbnf`), `enc` the encoding (through `spec/pdp64/pdp64.hbnf`: hbnf's decoder), `spec` the numbers the generators read (constants, the manual; hbnf does not read a part that names only `spec`). A section is in every grammar it names, so it must be valid in each: the words of a family (asm, spec) and its encoding (enc) are two parts. A section with no `targets` line is an error.
4. **Rules.** A rule is defined once: a second `name =` in any part is an error naming both files and lines. (hbnf itself would let the later definition win; here nothing overrides.) The one way to extend a rule is hbnf's `name =/ alternatives`, which adds alternatives to the rule an earlier section defined, whichever part defined it (`sysreg =/ "hvctl"@13`, `forms =/ "CRY1"`); an `=/` with no earlier `=` is an error.
5. **Generated parts.** A section whose first line is `; GENERATED by <tool> ... do not edit` is owned whole by that tool: it writes its own files and no other, a person never edits it, and a hand-written section must not carry that line. The tools: `tools/encopt/addforms.py` (the forms, shapes and the words that no hand group numbers: `20-enc-forms`, `21-enc-shapes`, `80-mn-ext`), `tools/mkparts.py` (`10-enc-insn/prologue.hbnf`: `insn`, listing every `leaf-FORM-K` of every part; `90-mnemonic/epilogue.hbnf`: `mnemonic`, listing every group `mn-NAME` of the parts that name `asm`), `tools/grammars_d.py` (the indexes).
6. **The assembled result.** hbnf has `include` and no glob, so `tools/grammars_d.py --write` writes `spec/generated/index-enc.hbnf` and `index-asm.hbnf` (marked GENERATED): one `include` per section file of the parts of the target, in the three-pass order. The hand-edited masters `spec/pdp64/pdp64.hbnf` and `spec/pdp64-asm.hbnf` hold the directives and include their index; they define no rule. Nothing is copied or patched into `build/`: hbnf reads the masters as they are.
7. **What reads the directory.** Every tool reads all the parts, whatever their targets say (`tools/encgram.py` `load()`/`read_rules()`, `tools/mkgrammar.py` `enums()`, through `tools/grammars_d.py`): `mkgrammar.py` (constants, names, `pdp64-groups.h`), `mkenc.py` (the codec tables), `mkmanual.py`, `gen-llvm-td.py`, `vec-glance.py`, `mk_vectors.py`, `mk_asm_tests.py`, `mk_ld_tests.py`, `check-drift-enc.py`, the checks. The constants (`build/pdp64-consts.h`) are made from the hand-written sections that name `spec`.

### How a part is added

1. Pick the number: it is the load order. Low numbers come first inside a pass; the existing parts are `00-ascii 01-common 05-registers 10-enc-insn/ 20-enc-forms 21-enc-shapes 22-enc-vocabulary 23-enc-registers 30-mn-core 35-mn-numbers 40-mn-system 41-sysreg 42-conditions 50-system-v1 51-iommu-v1 60-asm-syntax 80-mn-ext 90-mnemonic/`; a new family takes a free number (`70`, `71`...).
2. Write the file(s), each beginning with `; targets: ...`: the words (`mn-NAME = "word"@N | ...`; targets `asm spec`), system registers (`sysreg =/ "name"@N`), the syntax (`asm`), the encoding (`forms =/ "NAME"`, `form-NAME`, `leaf-NAME-K`, `sub-NAME-K`, a new shape rule; target `enc`). The example `98-example-words.hbnf.disabled` and `99-example-enc.hbnf.disabled` is the smallest complete family: a group of two words, a system register and one leaf; copy them without `.disabled` to try them.
3. Run any build (`tools/build-as64.sh`, `tools/mkgrammar.py`) or `python3 tools/mkparts.py && python3 tools/grammars_d.py --write`: `insn`, `mnemonic` and the indexes are written again. If a family is also optimised, change `docs/codegen-data/encoding/vecfam.py` and use the generator workflow below instead.
4. `python3 tools/mkenc.py --md`, `tools/check-grammar-enc.sh --update` (review the diff of `tests/enc/generated.sha256`), `python3 docs/codegen-data/encoding/mk_vectors.py mk_asm_tests.py mk_ld_tests.py`, `tools/check-all.sh`.

A hand-written leaf takes a first-parcel prefix that is free: `tools/mkenc.py` checks that the prefixes are a prefix code and names the overlap; `tools/encopt/addforms.py` chooses the prefixes of the forms it owns without knowing a hand-written family's, so when the optimiser is run again after a hand family was added, a collision is reported by `mkenc.py`, and the hand prefix is changed. The words of a hand-written leaf must be numbered by a hand-written group `mn-NAME` (`mkenc.py` fails, naming the words, otherwise). The operand forms of a hand-written family are a catalogue entry and a form rule in its own part; the encoding optimiser does not see them (its catalogue file for a hand-written family, next to `vecfam.py`, is not built: what is implemented is the part with its own leaves).

### What the checks say

`tools/check-grammar-enc.sh` runs `tools/mkparts.py --check` and `tools/grammars_d.py --check`: a part that is not numbered, a repeated number, a section with no or a wrong `targets` line, a rule defined twice (both files named), an `=/` with nothing to extend, a directive outside a prologue, a hand-written part with the GENERATED line, a generated part that is not what its tool writes, a stale index (a part added, removed or renamed without regenerating), a master that defines a rule or does not include its index. Then hbnf must accept both masters, the parts of `addforms.py` must be what it writes, and the generated tables must be the ones in `tests/enc/generated.sha256`.

### Future direction and the request to hbnf

`tools/grammars_d.py` and `tools/mkparts.py` are deliberately thin, text-level stopgaps. The owner's intent is that hbnf parses each part itself and merges prologues, grammars and epilogues into the sections of one grammar; then the two scripts and the indexes go away and no part changes. The request: a directive `include dir "grammars.d"` (or `--parts=DIR`), which reads the directory by the contract above (layout, three passes, `NN` order); the error cases name the part and the file (`grammars.d/22-x/epilogue.hbnf:7: rule foo is defined twice, first in grammars.d/20-y.hbnf:3`; `grammars.d/NN-x/: a part directory holds at least one of prologue.hbnf, grammar.hbnf, epilogue.hbnf`; `a directive after a rule in prologue.hbnf`; `=/ of a rule no earlier section defines`); and a way to read the `; targets:` line (or `include dir "grammars.d" for enc`) so that one directory serves several grammars. The collecting rules (`insn`, `mnemonic`) would then be an epilogue rule that names "every rule matching `leaf-*`" or "every rule matching `mn-*`", which hbnf has no notation for; until it has one, `tools/mkparts.py` writes them.

## The facts about the words (errata 183, 184)

The grammar is the source of truth for the names, the encodings and the operand shapes. What a grammar cannot hold (the meaning, the wave a word came from, whether the LLVM back end carries it, the executor's handler, the assembler's lane-type class and the other facts that other tools keep by hand) is in `spec/facts/`: git-controlled TOML, one file for each group of words (`vec.toml` for the vec family, `core.toml` for every other instruction), one table for each base mnemonic. **The base mnemonic is the key**: the part of a word before the first dot (`vdacc` for `vdacc.b`, `vdacc.bu`), so a grammar word needs no annotation to find its table. The fields of each file are described in its header.

* `tools/facts.py` reads the files (`facts.load('vec')`) and `tools/facts.py --check` (in `check-all.sh`) fails if the grammar has a base with no table, or a table names a base the grammar no longer has; and, for the vec family, if a table lacks a field, if a base names a manual entry that no base defines, if the executor has no `case` for a base's handler or has a `case` that no base names, if no test of `tests/sim` or `tests/qemu` runs the base (a test that only assembles it does not count), if a table says the compiler selects the base (`compiler`) and the named file of `llvm/target/PDP64/` does not spell it (or `llvm/vector-usage.md` says the compiler emits a base whose table has no `compiler`), or if a row of the gate tables (`tools/gates/silicon-total.py`, `lat-table.py`) names a word that is not a base.
* Generated from the facts, with identical output to what the hand lists wrote: `tools/vec-glance.py` (the meaning, the wave), `tools/gen-llvm-td.py` (the `llvm` flag), `tools/manual/isa_vec.py` (the manual's vec entries: `entry` and the text on the first base of each entry), `tools/gen-vec-names.py` (the executor's name table of the vec family, `common/pdp64exec_vecbases.inc`, field `exec`; and the assembler's lane-type classes, `as64/asmvecclass.inc`, fields `asm`, `asm_no2d`, `asm_fsum`, which `vec_typecheck` of `as64/as64.c` selects its rule by), `tools/gen-core-names.py` (the executor's name table of the core instructions, `common/pdp64exec_corehdefs.inc`, from `spec/facts/core.toml`) and `llvm/bench/usage.py` (the groups, `group`, and the reasons, `usage_why`, `usage_supported`). The generated `.inc` files are copied to the QEMU target by `qemu/sync.sh`; `--check` of each generator fails if a file on disk differs. The executor's semantics (the `case` code), the enum of its handlers, the compiler's selection code and the gate tables stay hand-written.
* To add a base word: add it to the vec family (`docs/codegen-data/encoding/vecfam.py`, the workflow below), then add its table to `spec/facts/vec.toml`, run `tools/gen-vec-names.py`, give it a test; the checks say what is missing. For a core word, add its table to `spec/facts/core.toml` and run `tools/gen-core-names.py`.
* If a record is ever to cover words of several bases, or a word is to name a table other than its base, the grammar rule can carry a comment `; fact: NAME`, which a tool reads (hbnf ignores comments); no rule needs it yet.

## The generator workflow

1. Change what decides the forms: `docs/codegen-data/encoding/vecfam.py` (the vec, tile and GEMM family), or, for a change in the hand-written part, a hand-written part of `grammars.d/`.
2. `python3 tools/encopt/addforms.py --grammar-out spec/grammars.d` (with `--drop NAME` for a removed form) writes the three generated parts, then `python3 tools/mkparts.py && python3 tools/grammars_d.py --write` (any build does it); run on a tree with no change it writes the same bytes.
3. `python3 tools/mkenc.py --md`, then `tools/check-grammar-enc.sh --update` (review the diff of `tests/enc/generated.sha256`), then `python3 docs/codegen-data/encoding/mk_vectors.py`.
4. `tools/check-all.sh`.

The optimiser (`docs/codegen-data/encoding/`) stays as the independent oracle: `tools/check-enc.py` lays the grammar's forms out again with the Python model (`enc.py`) and requires the
same prefixes and bit positions as the table, then compares the C codec with the model on boundary vectors of every mnemonic of every form. It is also the design tool that writes a
new grammar (`export_forms.py` and `export.py --write-grammar` call `write_hbnf.py`, which writes the generated files above); the hand-written parts are edited by hand. `tools/mkenc.py` verifies, independently of the optimiser, that the prefixes are a prefix
code, that each leaf covers its 16n bits exactly once and that no first parcel decodes to two leaves.

## Changing an encoding (the workflow)

1. Change the grammar: a form or leaf is generated, so change what generates it (`vecfam.py`, then the generator workflow above) and never edit a generated part; by hand only the hand-written parts of `spec/grammars.d/`. The shape of a leaf (a `leaf-FORM-K` rule with its prefix, fields and `sub-FORM-K` list, listed in `insn`; a form is a `form-` rule and a number in the catalogue) is what the generator writes. The encoding optimiser reads the compiler's assembly for the 841-file corpus and writes the same generated files whole (`tools/encopt`; its translation from the Python original to C, with optimal algorithms, is a work item, and it is to be run again once the decisions that affect the opcodes are made and the mnemonics are settled; see `handoff.md`, the order of work). The Python scripts of the two-grammar period in `docs/codegen-data/encoding/` are the original and are history: those that read two grammars by tag run from the git tag `pre-code-removal` (`docs/codegen-data/encoding/README.md`). Compare a new grammar with `diff -ru spec OUTDIR` and copy what is wanted (the grammar `pdp64/pdp64.hbnf`; `pdp64-enc.hbnf` changes with the shapes the forms use). The hot-register tables of the grammar are the ABI and are kept unless the author decides otherwise.
2. `tools/mkenc.py --md` rewrites the tables of `spec/pdp64/tables.md`.
3. `tools/check-grammar-enc.sh` fails, naming the generated files that changed; after reading the differences, `tools/check-grammar-enc.sh --update` records them in `tests/enc/generated.sha256`.
   The same script runs hbnf on the grammar (syntax, references, widths), compares hbnf's decoder with the codec built from the new tables on 262,144 parcel sequences,
   and checks tables.md. If the leaves do not form a prefix code, or a field has the wrong width, `mkenc.py` or `encgram.py` say which rule.
4. `tools/check-enc.sh` (the codec against the Python model, the drift of every mnemonic through the assembler), then `tools/check-all.sh`. The vectors and assembler corpora derived from the
   forms (`tests/enc/vectors.txt`, the ld64 and assembler tests `tests/ld`, `tests/asm`) are rewritten by `docs/codegen-data/encoding/mk_vectors.py`, `mk_asm_tests.py`, `mk_ld_tests.py` (they read the grammar through `encgram`)
   and checked in (run all three after a change: the tests record nothing by hand, but their expected words are the model's); `asm_vs_model.py` (needs the corpus and `as64 --dump-lens` of each file) compares the real
   assembler with the model. What the checks record of the grammars is derived from them (the number of forms, leaves and unassigned first parcels is not written down anywhere); what stays written down is the ABI:
   the hot-register tables (`tools/check-drift-enc.py`).
5. Anything that depends on a layout (a compiler back end, QEMU, a disassembler) is generated from the same tables or the same grammar; if it is not, that is the bug.

The mnemonics are those of the original grammar (aliases included) plus the words only the new encodings have, which the rule `mn-ext` of `grammars.d/80-mn-ext.hbnf` lists (`mkenc.py` checks the list against the words the leaves use) as the group `mn-ext`
of the grammar as64 reads (`spec/pdp64-asm.hbnf` over the parts that name `asm`): `maskeqz masknez bsel aeses aesesm aesds aesdsm aesks1 aesks2 aesim sha256* sha512* msha256 msha512 maesctr addv subv mulv`, the user-qualified moves `mov.user ...` and `mcpy.ur mcpy.uw`, and the fused `movcall popmret popret mov2`. They have no number in the original numbering of `pdp64-mnemonics.hbnf`.

## The model in two paragraphs

An instruction is one to five 16-bit parcels (little-endian in memory); its length is a function of parcel 0 alone. The top bits of parcel 0 are a prefix of 1 to 16 bits
that selects a *leaf*: a group of mnemonics with one form (operand shapes and field widths); a sub-opcode field inside the leaf (binary-aligned) selects the mnemonic.
Register fields start at bit 0 of parcel 0 and go up (the last register operand lowest), displacements and immediates fill the rest, the widest field last, so in the later parcels.
No bit is padding: an unused sub-opcode, or a hot-register index beyond the table, decodes as an illegal instruction. The first-parcel patterns that match no prefix are illegal instructions (the count is in `tables.md` and manual 9.1.5).

The assembler picks, for each instruction, the first form in the table's order (shorter first, then the order of the catalogue `forms`) whose fields can hold the operands, and relaxes
(lengths only grow; every distance is measured on the layout of the previous pass, which is the iteration of the model). A branch, call or `d(PC)` operand is measured in bytes
from the first parcel of the instruction; the field counts parcels for branches and calls (unit 2) and bytes for the memory forms (unit 1). An unresolved symbol needs a form with a field
of 32 bits or more and a relocation; the relocation types are those of `as64/relocs.h`, the linker finds the field through the codec.

## Operand notation and the choices the tables imply

* `(r)+` and `-(r)` without a stride write stride 0, which means the size of the access (8 outside the moves); `(r)+8` writes 8.
* `pushm`, `popm`, `popmret` take a register mask; a mask is the low 32 bits of the number (so `$-16740608` is a mask of r31...).
* `setCC ra, rb, rd` and `setCC ra, $imm, rd` (an 8-bit signed immediate): 4 bytes. `trap $n`, `spl $n`, `hint $n`: one parcel with an 8-bit number.
* ATOM: three register fields (`aadd`... `sc`; `cas` shares one register between the expected value and the old value) and, for `ll`, two; the memory address is `(Ra)`, and the destination is written last: `aadd rs, (ra), rd`, `ll (ra), rd`.
* `cbCC` for which no form reaches (an external label, a target beyond 16 MB) is assembled as the opposite `cbCC` over a `br` to the target; neither writes anything (the machine has no flags), so the program is the same, longer by the `br`. The forms are two, four and six bytes: the compare of `eq` or `ne` with zero is two bytes, a small immediate or two registers four, and six bytes hold a 12-bit immediate (64 KB), a 16-bit immediate (4 KB) or two registers (16 MB), for all ten conditions alike (manual 9.10.5; the generated tables list every form and its reach). `fcbCC` is the floating-point twin, four bytes or six.
* `mov $imm, rd` whose constant fits no shorter form is the ten-byte form (form `MOVQ64`, five parcels) with the 64-bit immediate.  One word names an operation and never its length: the assembler chooses the form from the operands (`jmp label` is `br label`), and `.encode N` forces a form of at least N parcels.
* The 22 meaningless shapes the original assembler accepts (`clr/com/inc/dec/neg/asl/asr/jmp/call/pop/jmpi/calli $imm`, `mov.s8/mov.s16/mov.s32 r,$imm`, `lea $imm,r`, `sob r,$imm`, ...) are refused.
* The assembler never fuses. The fused instructions are ordinary mnemonics with operands: `movcall rs, rd, label` (`mov rs,rd ; call label`, the target measured from the first parcel of the fused instruction),
  `popmret $mask`, `popret rd`, `mov2 ra, rb, rc, rd` (`mov ra,rb ; mov rc,rd`, all four in the hot-8 table). Choosing them is the compiler's job.
* `extb/extzb/insb` have 6-bit position and width: a width of 64 is refused. The MESC words (`addm... bism`) exist only in the 3-parcel memory forms (the optimiser selected no narrower one: no use in the corpus).

## The hot-register tables (part of the ABI, docs/abi.md)

A 3-bit register field in a one-parcel form is an index, not a register number: `r0 r1 r8 r2 sp r3 r9 r10`.
There are also 4-bit fields: `r0 r1 r8 r2 sp r3 r9 r10 r11 r4 r15 r5 r24 r25 r26 r6` (`r24`-`r26` are hot registers; their roles are in the ABI, appendix D);
A different register allocation changes the sizes, not the correctness. `mov2` needs all four registers in the 3-bit table.

## Linker relaxation

`as64` marks an object (by default; `--no-relax` gives the plain object) (`e_flags` 0x10, `EF_PDP64_RELAX`; 0x20, `EF_PDP64_NOADJ_TEXT`, if its `.text` holds a distance that is not a relocation) and gives every
instruction that holds the address of a symbol a relocation, local symbols too, and every padding of `.text` an `R_PDP64_ALIGN` (type 6; addend = padding bytes << 32 | alignment).
`ld64` then picks, for each such instruction, the shortest form of the codec that reaches, after laying the sections out, and moves everything behind it (the lengths
only grow from the shortest form, so the iteration ends; paddings are recomputed from the positions).  The reach of the forms is in the tables (the reach of each form is in `spec/pdp64/tables.md` and the manual's dictionary; manual 9.5.5 states the reach of the four-byte `call`, `lea` and branch forms).  Objects made with `--no-relax` are linked as before; `ld64 --no-relax` links relaxation objects exactly as the assembler
laid them out.  Details, the argument that it ends and is right, what it cannot do, the checks and the measurements: `docs/relaxation.md`.


## The manual's charts

`tools/mkmanual.py` draws the bit-layout charts of the manual from the grammar (`tools/manual/layouts.py`); a change to a field in the grammar changes the chart, and `tools/mkmanual.py --check` fails until the manual is regenerated.

## Width and kind suffixes (61-asm-suffixes.hbnf)

The width and the kind of an operation are a dotted suffix of the mnemonic: `.s8 .s16 .s32 .s64`, `.u8 .u16 .u32 .u64` and `.f16 .f32 .f64` (the number is in bits; `.f8` is refused as no such format). A bare word is the default, 64 bits, and the same word as its explicit `.s64`, `.u64` or `.f64` spelling. No operand carries a mark, except those of `fcvt`, which has two formats and writes them on its operands, source first (`fcvt r1.f16, r2.f32`): an operand mark `.s8 ... .f64` is part of a register's token (`markop` in `60-asm-syntax.hbnf`), so `x.s8`, `buf.u8` and a label `r1.f32:` are names, and as64 refuses a mark on every other word. The part `61-asm-suffixes.hbnf` holds the one table: `map-rows` lists every accepted spelling (`mov.s8`, `aadd.s32`, `fadd.f32`, `fcvt.s32`, `mov.user.u16` ...) with the word of the encoding it is (`movb`, `aaddl`, `fadd32`, `fcvtls`, `movzw.u`) and flags, and marks with a `*` the spelling that the disassembler and the compilers print for that word; `map-marks` lists, for each conversion word of the encoding, the mark of its first operand and of its second (as64 looks the pair of marks up; the disassembler, pcc and LLVM write them; a word with a short form, `fcvt.s64`, `fcvt.u64`, `fcvt.s32`, is printed with it); `map-words` lists the words of the older notations and the words of the encoding that no spelling stands for, which `instruction` refuses (`movb`, `faddf` and `fadd32` are unknown mnemonics). `tools/mkparts.py` writes the group `mn-surface` (`62-mn-surface.hbnf`) from the rows, one whole-word keyword for each spelling, because the grammar's keywords are whole words and have no rule "word, dot, kind"; `tools/mkgrammar.py` writes the rows to `build/x/pdp64-marks.h` for `as64` (a spelling becomes the word of the encoding; a kind a word does not have is refused with a message that names the word and the suffixes it takes) and `tools/encdis.c` (a word becomes its spelling), and the renaming table to `build/pdp64-pcc-marks.h`, which `tools/export-pcc-headers.sh` copies to the pcc back end; `tools/marks.py` reads the same rows for the tools that write source (the generators of the tests, the drift check) and `tools/gen-llvm-td.py` writes the strings of the LLVM back end from them. The encoding itself, its tables and `spec/pdp64/tables.md` do not change.
