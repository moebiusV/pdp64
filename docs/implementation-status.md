# Implementation status

What the implementations (the simulator `sim64`, the QEMU model, the reference executor, the generated tables, the FPGA plan) do or lack today, as against what the architecture decides. The manual describes the architecture as it is to be (the author, 2026-11-10); remarks about the state of the implementation were moved here from the chapters. One bullet per item: chapter, statement or topic, what the implementation does, what the decision says, the errata number.

* **Chapter 6 (kernel), 6.1 how to read and 6.11 intro.** No pdp64 kernel exists; the pdp64 pseudocode of 6.11 and 6.12 is an illustration and untested. The decision (errata 107): the pseudocode is the assistant's illustration. Removed text: "no pdp64 kernel exists (the first operating system is OpenBSD, 6.12.3, and none of it is written), so none of it is tested" and "It is not from a pdp64 kernel (there is none) and is untested."
* **Chapter 6, 6.3 table, vector 0.** The QEMU machine does not use vector 0. Removed text: "reset (not used by the QEMU machine)". Decision: vector 0 is reset (errata 107).
* **Chapter 6, 6.6 walk and 6.6.1a levels.** The walker of the QEMU model (`qemu/target-pdp64/mmu.c`) implements the walk with three levels and also faults on a leaf not aligned to its size; the simulator and the emulator implement three levels only. Removed text: "was checked against the walker of the QEMU model (`qemu/target-pdp64/mmu.c`); a leaf not aligned to its size is also a page fault there", "what the simulator and the emulator implement", "The simulator and the emulator implement three levels." Decision: three, four or five levels, discoverable at run time (errata 96).
* **Chapter 6, 6.6.2 and 6.7.1 physical address.** The implementation has a 40-bit physical address and a page number in bits 12 to 39; the simulator, the QEMU model and `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf` use 40 bits. Removed text: "40 in the present implementation", "in place in the present implementation", "The simulator, the QEMU model and `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf` use 40 bits, the minimum." Decision: 40 to 64 bits (errata 102).
* **Chapter 6, 6.7 memory type and bit 39.** The simulator and the QEMU model still compare the memory type of a page with bit 39 of its physical address (errata 60 and 65); that check belongs to the present implementation. Removed text: "They also still compare the memory type of a page with bit 39 of its physical address (the rule of errata 60 and 65 that errata 97 removed); that check belongs to the present implementation and goes when the implementation catches up." and, from 6.15, "the check of the memory type against bit 39 that the implementation still performs (6.7), which errata 97 removed from the architecture (appendix B)". Decision: no address bit selects device space (errata 97).
* **Chapter 6, 6.8 translation buffer size.** The FPGA plan (`docs/pdp64-system-architecture.md`) assumes a four-way set-associative buffer of 64 entries. Removed text (chapter and the note of the figure `kern-tlb` in `tools/manual/kernfigs.py`): "The FPGA plan ... assumes a four-way set-associative buffer of 64 entries" and "the FPGA plan assumes 4-way, 64 entries (section 6.14)"; from 6.14: "the plan for the translation buffer on the FPGA, four-way and 64 entries". Decision: size and organisation are the implementation's choice.
* **Chapter 6, 6.8 ASID and flush.** The reference executor flushes the whole translation buffer on `MTASID`, and on a write to `PTBR`, `ASID` or `MMUCTL`, which defeats the address-space tags. Removed text: "The reference executor does not yet behave this way: its `MTASID` flushes the whole buffer" (the entry of `MFSR` in chapter 9 stating the flush stays in the chapter as a disagreement between two specification texts). Decision: errata 71 and 107(g); the tags exist so that a switch flushes nothing.
* **Chapter 6, 6.9.1 split translation.** `KTBR` is not implemented; the simulator and the QEMU model have one table base, `PTBR`, and test only that the high bits equal bit 38. The work needed is a system-register number, the walker and the tests. Removed text: "It is **not yet implemented**: the simulator and the QEMU model have one table base, `PTBR`, and test only that the high bits equal bit 38. The work needed is a system-register number, the walker and the tests." and, from 6.14, "implements neither the split of 6.9.1 nor the windows". Decision: errata 71 (the split accepted); the number of `KTBR` is open (errata 107(c)).
* **Chapter 6, 6.9.2 direct-mapping windows.** The windows are stored and have no translation effect. The statement 6.9.2 keeps this (it is the specification's own statement); the sentence "The manual stores them and does nothing with them" was reworded to "The registers are stored and have no defined effect".
* **Chapter 6, 6.10.4 and 6.10.1 user guard.** The simulator's status word has no `UA` bit: its valid mask `PSW_VALID` covers the bits 0 to 11 and `STUA` and `CLUA` are not executed; the reference executor treats the qualified move `.U` as a plain move. Removed text: "and the reference executor treats the qualified move as a plain move", "The simulator's status word has no `UA` bit yet (its valid bits stop at bit 11); the guard of 6.10 is decided (errata 43) and not implemented." and, from 6.15, "and that the simulator's status word has no `UA` bit (6.14)". Decision: `UA` is bit 12 (errata 43); `.U` is proposed (the study).
* **Chapter 6, 6.13.8 `TRAPCC`.** The reference executor takes vector 16 with `EVA` the address of the `TRAPCC`. Removed text: "The reference executor takes vector 16 (the check vector of the study) with `EVA` the address of the `TRAPCC`; both are assumptions"; the chapter now says the vector and `EVA` value are assumptions and the vector number a proposal.
* **Chapter 6, 6.12.2 the QEMU machine's start.** The QEMU machine starts at the entry point of the ELF given with `-kernel`, with the kernel stack at the top of memory; the option `kernel-mode=on` turns off the system-call stand-in of user-mode programs so that `TRAP` enters the kernel. Removed text: the statement 6.12.2 of the kernel chapter, tagged (D) for the QEMU machine. It describes the implementation, not the architecture; the architectural reset is in 4.4.3 and 6.12.1.
* **Chapter 6, 6.14 QEMU walker.** "the walker of the QEMU model (`qemu/target-pdp64/mmu.c`), which implements the walk of 6.6 with three levels, the reserved-bit and alignment checks and software accessed and dirty bits" was removed from the list of what the chapter was checked against.
* **Chapter 2 (bus), 2.3.1 physical address width.** The simulator and the emulator implement a 40-bit physical address. Removed text: " (the simulator and the emulator implement 40)". Decision: 40 to 64 bits (errata 102, 118).
* **Chapter 2, 2.3.6 device addresses.** The simulator and the emulator place their devices in the upper half of a 40-bit space, from `0x8000000000`, and check the memory type of a page against that bit. Removed text: "The simulator and the emulator place their devices in the upper half of a 40-bit space, from `0x8000000000`, and check the memory type of a page against that bit; these two arrangements belong to the present implementation, not to the architecture." Decision: no address bit selects device space (errata 97, 102, 118).
* **Chapter 2, 2.7 intro (virtio).** Claimed that the same virtio devices run in QEMU, the simulator and the FPGA; no source in the repository was found for the simulator and the FPGA. Removed text: ", and the same devices run in QEMU, the simulator and the FPGA". Decision: virtio across the board (errata 95, 118).
* **Chapter 2, 2.11 IOMMU.** No IOMMU unit exists in any implementation. Removed text: "Until the unit exists, a device addresses all of memory and a user-space driver can direct its device to write anywhere (5.10.2, 5.13.2)." Reworded in the chapter as the architecture without translation ("Without translation a device addresses all of memory ..."). Decision: an IOMMU is part of the design; its format and place are open (errata 75, 85, 118).
* **Chapter 4 (boot), 4.0 how to read.** No pdp64 boot ROM, first stage or second stage exists; the QEMU machine loads the kernel directly (6.12.2), so none of the chapter's pseudocode is tested. Removed text: "no pdp64 boot ROM, first stage or second stage exists (the QEMU machine loads the kernel directly, 6.12.2), so none of it is tested." Decision: the sketches are illustrations of the proposed boot (4.3 to 4.10, errata 108). Errata 119.
* **Chapter 4, 4.5 first stage size.** Whether the virtio-blk reader fits beside the block list in 446 bytes is untried because nothing has been assembled. Removed text: "unknown: nothing has been assembled" (now "is not established"). Decision: how the first stage reads blocks is open (4.13 b). Errata 119.
* **Chapter 4, 4.9 shim sketch.** No shim code exists. Removed text: "(an illustration; no such code exists)" and, in 4.11, "A proposal and a decision, no code." Decision: a shim is possible, not built (errata 109). Errata 119.
* **Chapter 4, 4.10 monitor size.** The monitor's size is an estimate because nothing has been assembled. Removed text: "(an estimate; nothing has been assembled)" now "(an estimate)". Decision: 4.10.1 is proposed (P). Errata 119.
* **Figure generator `tools/manual/bootfigs.py`, shim figure.** Caption "Nothing is built; this illustrates the size of the job (4.9)." now reads "This illustrates the size of the job (4.9)." Errata 119.
* **Chapter 1, 1.5.3 QEMU and the encoding.** QEMU executes the encoding of the pdp64 (since the removal of the earlier iterations, errata 122); the C test programs pass on the simulator and on QEMU. Decision: the QEMU target is to reach parity with `sim64` (errata 84).
* **Chapter 10, 10.4.3 the present toolchain.** The assembler and linker exist; the assembler lacks the atomics and the linker lacks shared libraries; the pcc back end passes 516 C tests on the simulator and on QEMU; the LLVM back end has a machine-code layer for the original encoding of the earlier iterations (no longer checked, since the assembler no longer produces that encoding: `llvm/README.md`); the simulator and QEMU run a kernel-mode machine with exceptions, a memory-management unit, a timer and a console; no part of OpenBSD is ported. Removed as a statement of the chapter (it described the state of the tools). Decision: the finish line is 10.4.1 (errata 84).
* **Chapter 10, 10.1.8 synthesis.** No core has been synthesised (the sentence moved to appendix H with the board plan, 10.1.2 and 10.1.8).
* **Chapter 5 (drivers), intro and 5.3.1.** No pdp64 driver has been written, so every pdp64 listing of the chapter is an illustration and untested. Neither QEMU's pdp64 machine nor `sim64` has a virtio device yet; the statement that "the same devices run in QEMU, `sim64` and the FPGA" is an aim. Removed text: "No pdp64 driver has been written: ... except where it names a test that exists (5.14)" and "(Neither QEMU's pdp64 machine nor `sim64` has a virtio device yet: 5.14.)".
* **Chapter 5, 5.7 console and timer in the implementation.** `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf` defines, for the QEMU machine and `sim64`, a timer (a 64-bit free-running counter at 10,000,000 ticks a second of QEMU's virtual clock, a 64-bit compare register and a control register, interrupting at level 6 on vector 32 while the counter is at or above compare) and a console in the spirit of the KL11 (four 64-bit registers `RXCS`, `RXDB`, `TXCS`, `TXDB`, READY in bit 7, interrupt-enable in bit 6, level 4, vectors 33 receive and 34 transmit), at the fixed addresses `0x8000000000` and `0x8000001000` behind physical-address bit 39 of errata 60, which errata 97 withdrew; the specification and the QEMU machine have not been changed. No device tree exists in the repository. `decisions-pending.md` F11 leans to a 16550-style UART and F12 to "a small device tree for these (timer, interrupt controller, UART), PCI Express enumeration for the rest". `tests/qemu/con-tx.S` and `con-rx.S` do in assembly what the console illustration does; `tests/qemu/mini-kernel.S` moves the compare register by `PERIOD` 200000 (20 ms at 10 MHz). The decision: the form of the console, timer and entropy source is open (J.5.1).
* **Chapter 5, 5.9 vectors of the QEMU machine.** The vectors of the QEMU machine (32 timer, 33 and 34 console) are fixed wires, and `sim64`'s `-i N:LEVEL:VECTOR` option injects an interrupt by instruction count. Removed with the interrupt-controller discussion, which is open (J.6.3).
* **Chapter 5, 5.13 the tool chain.** The `FENCE` has no C intrinsic: the compiler's name table (`arch/pdp64/pdp64-names.h` in the pcc tree) knows the mnemonic and nothing else, so a driver today would call a function written in assembly (the question is J.5.5). For the repository's own fixed devices the offsets are generated constants, `pdp64-consts.h`, made from `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf`. The compiler's value reuse is tested not to merge two reads of a volatile (`tests/c-new/avail_volatile.c`, function `vtwice`). The two compilers are the C compiler `pcc` (working for pdp64/32 on the branch `enc-prefix`; the fallback system is Unix/32V) and the LLVM back end for OpenBSD, which has an assembler and a disassembler and no code generator yet (`handoff.md`), so no driver of the chapter can be compiled for the pdp64 by a compiler that exists for OpenBSD. Removed text: the sentences of 5.13 that said so.
* **Chapter 5, 5.14 testing a driver (the whole section).** `sim64` has no device and no bus: `-K` starts the System v1 kernel-mode machine with flat physical memory and no MMU, `-i N:LEVEL:VECTOR` raises an interrupt request once N instructions have started, `wfe` jumps to the next pending request (`tests/sim/sys-irq.S`, `sys-wfe.S`). QEMU's `pdp64-virt` machine with `kernel-mode=on` has the fixed timer and console, an interrupt controller that is two wires, the vector table at `VBR + 8 x vector` and an MMU; its tests are `tests/qemu/con-tx.S`, `con-rx.S`, `timer.S` and `mini-kernel.S`, run by `tools/check-qemu.sh`. Not implemented anywhere in the repository: a PCI Express root complex, configuration space, a virtio device, an MSI-X target, an interrupt controller beyond the two fixed wires, an IOMMU, a block device, a network device (a search of `qemu/`, `sim64/`, `common/` and `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf` for `pci`, `virtio`, `msi` and `iommu` finds nothing). QEMU's own `hw/virtio` holds the virtio devices and the PCI transport; a pdp64 machine that attached them would need a root complex model, an interrupt controller and an IOMMU model in `qemu/hw-pdp64/virt.c`; none is begun. Removed from the chapter as a description of the state of the tools.
* **Chapter 5, 5.15 and 5.16.** The ledger remarks of 5.15 (the ledger still listed the virtio-only rewrite as TO DO and said `errata.md` ends at 93) and the closing list 5.16 "Still open" are removed; the open items are entries J.5.1 to J.5.6 and J.6.3.
* **Chapter 9, the dictionary entries (the Status line).** Each entry of the instruction dictionary used to end with a Status line saying whether the reference executor (`common/pdp64exec.c`, shared by `sim64` and QEMU; `common/pdp64exec_enc.inc` for the prefix-coded encodings, run by `sim64` only) implements the instruction. The line is gone from the manual (`tools/mkmanual.py`, `render_entry`). What it said, as of 2026-11-10: the executor does not implement the atomic group, the deferred-fault, user-qualified, AES, SHA, `MSHA256`, `MSHA512` and `MAESCTR` words (they trap as unimplemented instructions), and runs a `.U` move as a plain move; it treats `ICINV` and `ICINVA` as no-ops and flushes the translation buffer on `MTASID`; it takes a `TRAPCC` that fires outside the kernel as the end of the program; the check vector has the number 16 in the executor and no name in `spec/pdp64-system.hbnf`; the reserved words `SYSPG0` to `SYSPG5` trap as unimplemented instructions. The index page of the dictionary no longer counts the instructions that the executor implements.
* **Chapter 9, `ABS`, `MIN`, `MINU`, `MAX`, `MAXU` (errata 98).** By decision they are assembler aliases. The grammars (`spec/pdp64-mnemonics.hbnf` and the encoding grammars), `as64`, `sim64`, pcc, QEMU and LLVM still treat the five words as native sub-opcodes of the arithmetic escape (`AESC`: `0x08` `abs`, `0x10` to `0x13` `min`, `minu`, `max`, `maxu`) until the expansion is decided (J.9.1); the entries of the manual show an alias and no encoding.
* **Chapter 9, the compiler items.** Listed as open in the old section 9.21.3 and kept here: the compiler's use of `MOVUW`, `MOVUL` and `MOVUQ`, division by 64-bit constants, loop-invariant code motion and live-range splitting.
* **Chapter 9, held-back entries and the grammars.** `tools/manual/isa_entries.py` (`HELD`) keeps out of the manual every dictionary entry whose notes open with the tag (P) or (S): `BSEL`, the deferred-fault loads, the user-qualified loads and the combination, the AES and SHA words, `MSHA256`, `MSHA512`, `MAESCTR` and `SYSPG0` to `SYSPG5`, 46 words in all. The grammars still carry them, so the counts of leaves and forms in 9.1.2, 9.1.4 and 9.19.1 are the grammars' counts and include the leaves of those words.
* **Appendix E, the LLVM assembler.** `llvm-mc` (`llvm/`, checked against `as64` by `llvm/check.sh`) accepts the syntax of appendix E with these exceptions: no literal pool (`=K` and `.pool`), no names that start with a digit, no non-ASCII names, and `.comm` needs its alignment written (`as64` implies 8); `(pc)` and `(dp)` without a displacement are accepted by `as64` (displacement 0) and by `llvm-mc`. The assembler grammar `spec/pdp64-asm.hbnf` is checked by `tools/check-asmsyntax.sh`, which generates the parser and runs `tests/asmsyntax.c` over it, and `tools/check-as64.sh` assembles `tests/asm/*.s` and compares the bytes with hand-worked expectations.
