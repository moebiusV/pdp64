## 1. The model

* The **block** is `vec Rs, Re` ... `vend`: the instructions between them run once for each **strip** of the **first stream** (the region `[Rs, Re)` of the pointer register `Rs`) until it is exhausted. A strip is `VL` elements of that stream, `1 <= VL <= the elements left`; the implementation chooses its strip (a power of two of at least 16 bytes, section 8) and the program never names the width and is never told it: there is no instruction that gives `VL` (`vlen` was removed, errata 173), and what a block computes is the same at every strip (section 8). The decided one-instruction prefix (R430) is the case of a block of one instruction without `vend`.
* **Hidden vector registers** `v0` to `v15` (the micro-kernel of the likely rows needs ten; the cost of 16 is in `vec-gates.md`), each as wide as the strip. They are **not ordinary registers**: they cannot be read or written by the scalar instructions, they are not saved at a context switch (R435), and **their contents do not survive from one strip to the next**, except for the accumulators that `vclr` names (section 4); a block that names accumulators has a strip of exactly 16 bytes (section 8.4).
* **Memory operands** are `(Ra)+` (the pointer moves past the strip: by `VL` elements of the access size, or by `VL` times the number of fields for a segment), `d(Ra)` (an offset, no move) and `d(Ra)+` (an offset, and the move). One memory operand for each instruction, as everywhere in the pdp64 (9.2).
* **Element suffixes**, the same as the scalar moves: `b w l q` for 8, 16, 32 and 64-bit integers, a following `u` for a load that zero-extends (`bu`, `wu`, `lu`), `f` and `d` for 32- and 64-bit floating point. A load or store may carry a second suffix, the **lane type** the strip is held in (`vld.bu.w` loads bytes into 16-bit lanes); a lane operation carries the lane type (`vadd.w`).
* **Order within a strip:** every load of a strip is done before any store of it becomes visible; strips happen one after the other, so a block whose streams do not overlap means what the same loop written one element at a time means, and a store and a load of the *same* element mean "read, then write"; **streams that overlap (a store over a load or another store at a different element) are unpredictable** (section 8.8: the result would depend on the strip; the sequential reading is withdrawn, errata 175).

## 3. How much encoding it needs

**[Now (errata 173, 2026-11-18)]: the family is in the grammar as 363 words in 23 forms** (`VBEG VEND VFIN VOP.VVV VOP.VVR VOP.VVI VDACC VTBLM VOP.VV VBSL VOP.VR VOP.VI VFMAB VACC VFIR VCLR VLD.PI VLD.B VST.PI VST.B VLDT VSTT VSTS.R`, from `docs/codegen-data/encoding/vecfam.py`; counted from `spec/pdp64/pdp64.hbnf` by `tools/vec-glance.py`), inside a prefix grammar of 181 forms and 239 leaves (errata 173 and the ledger). The words by stage: **384** when errata 161 added the family (the survey, `vec-survey.md`, counted 372 words: 12 fewer than the grammar had, errata 170), **347** after wave 1 (55 removed, 18 added; the survey's 335), **363** after wave 2 (16 added; the survey's 349 counted 14), and **363** now (`vlen.b` out, `vfin` in). The encodings are still the prefix optimiser's and **provisional** (errata 161; the numbers were compacted at waves 1 and 2 and again at errata 173): the optimiser is to run again on the corpus compiled by the LLVM back end. The estimate that stood here before the implementation is kept as the record of what was thought on 2026-11-17:

> About **40 base operations and 65 spellings** appear in the hand-written forms; with the five or six lane types of each, the **words are on the order of 200 to 300**. With three 4-bit vector-register fields (`v0` to `v15`) in a four-byte form, a leaf of 12 operand bits and a sub-opcode of 8 bits has a **prefix of 12 bits and costs 16 first-parcel patterns** (a 25th of 1%); a one-parcel hot form for the few most used (a load, a store, an add) would cost patterns in proportion to its frequency, which the compiled corpus does not yet show because **no compiler emits them** (the optimiser weighs a form by the instructions that use it). So the likely cost of the vector operations in first-parcel space is **tens of patterns, not thousands**, until the loops exist to be counted.

(The estimate of 200 to 300 words was low: the lane types times the memory types made 363. The loops now exist: the LLVM back end emits the family, section 5. The first-parcel cost of the 23 forms in the current grammar was not counted separately here.)

## 4. The restart, fault and number rules (the questions of section 5 of `gemm-forms.md`; all decided except item 2)

1. **The interrupt and the accumulators.** A block without `vclr` keeps nothing across strips beyond ordinary registers (the pointers, which move when a strip commits, and the reduction registers, floating-point sums included: section 8.5): an interrupt or a fault at a strip boundary needs no more than the registers, and the strip is **restartable**. A block with accumulators (a `vclr`, a `vfin`, a `vfmab`) has partial sums in hidden state. Decided (errata 172, 173; the owner: "hidden state is ok if we have precise exceptions and clean restarts"): the block **restarts from its entry**, the hardware keeping a hidden copy of the naming of the accumulators made at the `vec` and discarding the accumulators; the work lost is the block so far, bounded if the program bounds the block; its strip is 16 bytes on every implementation so that this hidden state is the program's own (section 8.4).
2. **The most strips of one block (still open).** To bound the work an interrupt can lose and the latency it can add, a block may be limited (the hardware stops the block at a strip boundary after N strips, with the pointers advanced, and the program runs `vec` again: a loop of blocks). To be determined with the first.
3. **What `VL` may be.** The strip is a power of two of at least 16 bytes (section 8), `VL` is the strip over the widest lane of the block, chosen for each block and not for each strip, the last strip short; the strip is the implementation's and invisible, except that the strip of a block with accumulators or a `vfmab` is exactly 16 bytes. A program never depends on `VL` because it can neither read it nor see it.
4. **Faults:** precise at the strip: the strip is annulled, `EPC` is the address of the `vec` (the block is a single instruction for the purpose of restart, like `MCPY`: 9.15), `EVA` and `ECAUSE` are those of **the first faulting access in the order of the elements** (the least element, then the first instruction: the same at every strip), and the pointers and the scalar result of a reduction that a handler reads are **opaque: an element boundary chosen by the implementation, no later than the faulting element; the operating system restarts by returning and never interprets them** (section 8.6; errata 173). *Tested* (`tests/sim/vec-restart.S`, errata 172 and 173, sim64 and QEMU, at the strips 16, 32 and 64): for every word of waves 1 and 2 and the older reductions, a fault in a load or a store stream or a misaligned pointer, in the first, a middle and the last strip and in a block of one element, with two streams faulting, and with a handler that reads only EPC, EVA and ECAUSE, leaves the stores of the strip out of memory, EPC at the `vec`, EVA and ECAUSE as the first fault in the element order, every pointer at the same element boundary no later than the faulting one, and the block resumed after `rti` gives the result of a run with no fault; a handler that overwrites v0 to v15 changes nothing. Two things the test showed and the executor now does: the accumulators that `vclr` names belong to one block (they are cleared when it ends), and a restart of a block with hidden accumulation takes the naming of its accumulators from the hardware's hidden copy made at the `vec`, so it is exact even when the handler ran vector code of its own. A `vclr` in the block itself needs neither. An interrupt is never taken inside a block in the executors (the block runs whole), so the asynchronous case is the fault case; the hidden state is not restored for a handler that names accumulators outside any block (open, errata 172).
5. **Overlap of the streams:** the loads of a strip before its stores (section 1); **overlapping streams are unpredictable** (section 8.8): the result would depend on the strip. (Before errata 173 this item said that the result of overlapping streams in different strips is the sequential one, which is itself strip dependent.)
6. **Numbers:** lane arithmetic wraps unless the mnemonic says saturating; floating-point lanes follow the scalar rules (IEEE 754, flags accumulated in `FPSR`, no traps: 9.12.3); a floating-point sum is in the canonical order of section 8.5.
7. **The assembler:** `vec`, `vend` and the mnemonics are words of the grammar; a vector register is written `v0` to `v15`; the lane and element suffixes follow the mnemonic with dots.

## 6. Wave 1 (errata 170): what is new, exactly

The words and forms are those of `vec-survey.md` 5.1, measured in `vec-silicon.md`; the owner accepted them ("i accept all your recommendations and leanings", R474). The rule of the page holds for every one: **nothing here keeps state from one strip to the next**; a result that is a register (`vdacc`, `vsad`) is committed when the strip commits, as `vacc` is, so a fault in a strip leaves it as it was at the start of the strip and the block restarts where it stopped. The strip of a block is as before: its widest lane type decides how many elements it has (the strip over the widest lane; the simulator's default strip is 32 bytes, `sim64 -S 16|32|64`; since errata 173 the strip is invisible and a block with accumulators has 16, section 8), and the last strip is short. Lane values are two's complement; "signed" and "unsigned" below name how a lane is read, and the result is truncated to the lane.

| word | forms | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `vavg.b .w`, `vavgu.b .w` | `vD, vA, vB` | `vD[i] = (vA[i] + vB[i] + 1) >> 1`, the sum made in a wider type (no wrap), signed (`vavg`) or unsigned (`vavgu`): the rounding average, round half up. `vavg.b` of 127 and 127 is 127; `vavgu.b` of 255 and 254 is 255. |
| `vmulh.w`, `vmulhu.w` | `vD, vA, vB \| Rs \| #imm` | `vD[i] = (vA[i] x vB[i]) >> 16` (arithmetic for `vmulh`, logical for `vmulhu`): the high 16 bits of the 32-bit product, no rounding. A register or an immediate is the 16-bit value (the low 16 bits of the register, the sign extended immediate) in every lane. |
| `vmulhr.w` | the same | the Q15 rounding multiply: `vD[i] = sat16((vA[i] x vB[i] + 2^14) >> 15)`, signed, round half up. **It saturates**: -32768 x -32768 is +2^30, which gives 32768 and is clamped to **32767** (the `sqrdmulh` rule; x86's `pmulhrsw` wraps it to -32768). No other product saturates. Decided for safety: a wrap would turn the largest positive value into the largest negative one without a word. |
| `vsarr.w .l`, `vshrr.w .l` | `vD, vA, vB \| Rs \| #imm` | the shift that rounds: `vD[i] = (vA[i] + 2^(s-1)) >> s` computed exactly (the sum is not wrapped), round half up, arithmetic (`vsarr`) or logical (`vshrr`); `s` is the unsigned value of the lane of the second operand (a register or immediate is the same count in every lane). `s = 0` leaves the lane; `s` equal to the lane width gives 0 for `vsarr` and 0 or 1 for `vshrr` (the top bit); `s` above the width gives 0 for both (the plain shifts `vsar` and `vshr` are unchanged: they fill with the sign and with zero). |
| `vmla.w .l` (new form) | `vD, vA, vB` | `vD[i] += vA[i] x vB[i]`, the low half, wrapping; the register form `vD, vA, Rs` is as before. The 8 and 64-bit lanes of `vmla` are gone. |
| `vdacc.b .bu .bsu .w` | `Rd, vA, vB` | `Rd += ` **the exact sum of the products of all the lanes of the strip**, in 64 bits: `.b` signed by signed bytes, `.bu` unsigned by unsigned, `.bsu` signed (`vA`) by unsigned (`vB`) `.w` signed halfwords. The strip is `VL` lanes of the source type, 32 bytes with byte lanes (no 32-bit lane is held, so the block's strip does not narrow to 8 lanes). Not wrapped to 32 bits. |
| `vsad.bu` | `Rd, vA, vB` | `Rd += the sum of \|vA[i] - vB[i]\|` over the unsigned byte lanes of the strip; the sum cannot overflow (at most 255 x VL). |
| `vtbl.b` | `vD, (Ra), vI` | byte lookup in a 16-entry table: `vD[i] = table[vI[i]]`, and **0 if `vI[i]` (unsigned) is 16 or more**. The table is exactly 16 bytes at `(Ra)`, read once for each strip, the same for every lane, independent of the strip's length. A fault in the 16 bytes is a fault of the strip. **There is no register form** (errata 173): the survey's `vtbl.b vD, vT, vI` took the table from the lanes of `vT`, so a short strip (or a strip of another width) truncated or changed it; a table that is in a register is stored and looked up from memory. A block with `vtbl.b` needs a strip of at least 16 byte lanes, which every implementation has (section 8). |
| `vsts.<lane>.<m> vS, (Ra)+, #sh` | `sh` = 0 to 63 | the narrowing store with a rounding shift: each lane is read as a signed integer, `y = (x + 2^(sh-1)) >> sh` (exact, arithmetic, round half up; `sh = 0`: `y = x`), then clamped to the range of `<m>` as `vsts` does (`bu`: 0 to 255, `b`: -128 to 127, ...) and stored. The form without `#sh` is as before. Integer lanes `w l q` only. |

**What the assembler checks** (`vec_typecheck`): `vavg vmulh vsarr` and the other lane words read both vectors as lanes of their own suffix; `vdacc`, `vsad` and `vtbl.b` read their vectors as lanes of the source type (`vtbl.b` bytes); `vsts` with or without `#sh` reads its register as the lane of its first suffix.

**What was removed, and how the numbers were handled.** `gemm.f`, `vtclr vtfmopa.f/.d vtld.f/.d vtst.f/.d vtadd.f/.d`, the 22 words of `vldx`, `vlen.w .l .q .f .d` (the one `vlen.b` that stayed in wave 1 was removed by errata 173, section 8), `vmul.q vmin.q vmax.q vminu.q vmaxu.q vabd.q vabd.f vabd.d vabdu.l vabdu.q`, `vmla.b vmla.q`, `vclr.b .w .q`, `vfir.b .q .d`: 55 words (the grammar had 384 words of the family at that stage, then 347; wave 2 made 363 and `vfin` replaced `vlen` without changing it, section 8). `as64` refuses them with its ordinary message for a word the grammar does not have (`expected one of ...`); the tile registers `t0` to `t7` and the gather operand `(Ra)[vI]` are no longer in the assembly grammar either (`t0` is an ordinary symbol everywhere). The encodings are provisional (errata 161), so the numbers were **compacted**: the forms were removed and the prefix grammar, the mnemonic numbers, the C tables, the QEMU copies and the golden vectors were written again from `vecfam.py`; every encoding of the first parcel moved (the prefix code was laid out again over fewer forms). No number was kept for a removed word.

**Restart, once more.** Fault at a strip: precise (section 4.4). The instructions of wave 1 read their sources and the table within the strip and write one vector register or one scalar register committed at the strip's end; `vclr` and its accumulators remain the only state that crosses a strip (and they stay ranked last).

## 7. Wave 2 (errata 171): compare, select and conversion, exactly

The words are those of `vec-survey.md` 5.1 items 10 and 11 with the measured silicon of `vec-silicon.md` (compare and select 3,801 NAND2 equivalents; the conversions 15,847, to be built only with the floating-point lanes, which the design has). **The rule of the page holds for every word: nothing here keeps state from one strip to the next**; each reads its sources inside the strip and writes one vector register, so a fault in a strip leaves everything as at its start (the instructions touch no memory and no scalar register). The strip of a block is as before (its widest lane type decides `VL`, the last strip is short; the lanes beyond `VL` are not stored and nothing reads them). `vbsl` has no lane type and does not count in the widest lane; the compares and `vcvt` have the lane type of their suffix. The two groups are separable in `vecfam.py`: `CMP` and `BSL` (12 words) and `CVT` (4 words, the 15.8 k) are lists that can be emptied independently and the grammar regenerated.

| word | forms | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `vceq.b .w .l .f`, `vcgt.b .w .l .f`, `vcgtu.b .w .l` | `vD, vA, vB \| Rs \| #imm` (the operand forms of the lane operations) | `vD[i]` = **all ones** if the relation holds between `vA[i]` and the second operand's lane, else **0**: equal, signed greater, unsigned greater. Integer lanes `b w l`; the `f` lane compares binary32. The result is an **integer lane of the size compared** (also for `.f`: a mask is bits, so `vand`, `vst.l` and `vbsl` take it; the assembler marks `vD` as 4-byte integer lanes). The floating-point compares are **ordered** and quiet: a NaN is not equal to anything (not to itself) and not greater, so the mask is 0; `-0.0` equals `+0.0`; the infinities order as numbers; **no flag is set in `FPSR`** (no vector lane operation touches it). A scalar register is the same value in every lane (low bits, or the binary32 bits for `.f`); an immediate is the 16-bit signed value sign extended and then cut to the lane (`vcgtu.b v1, v0, #-2` compares with 254), and for `.f` the integer value converted (`#-1` is `-1.0`). There is no compare for 64-bit lanes (no 64-bit integer lanes of `vmin vmax` exist either, wave 1) and no `vcgtu.f`. |
| `vbsl` | `vD, vM, vA, vB` | `vD = (vM & vA) \| (~vM & vB)`, bit by bit over the whole register: where a bit of the **mask is 1 the bit comes from `vA`**, where 0 from `vB`. The mask is the first source (as NEON `bsl`/`bit`, and the order of `x ? a : b`: `vbsl d, cond, a, b`). **`vD` may be the same register as `vM`, `vA` or `vB`** (all three are read before `vD` is written): no destructive-operand restriction, so the select never needs a copy. A mask that is not all ones or zero in a lane selects bits (it is a bit select, not a lane select). The assembler requires the mask and both operands to hold lanes of the same size (the kind, integer or floating point, is not checked: the operation is bitwise). |
| `vcvt.l.f`, `vcvt.lu.f` | `vD, vA` | each 32-bit lane: **signed** (`l`) or **unsigned** (`lu`) integer to binary32, **round to nearest even** (the scalar `FCVTLS` rule; no flag). `2^24 + 1` gives `2^24`; `2^24 + 3` (a tie) gives `2^24 + 4`; `0xffffffff` as `lu` gives `4294967296.0`. |
| `vcvt.f.l`, `vcvt.f.lu` | `vD, vA` | each 32-bit lane: binary32 to signed or unsigned 32-bit integer, **truncated toward zero** (not rounded to nearest: see below), then **saturated**. Signed (as the scalar `FCVTSL`): `>= 2^31` gives `0x7fffffff`, `< -2^31` gives `0x80000000`, **a NaN gives the largest value `0x7fffffff`**, `-0.0` gives 0. Unsigned (as the scalar `FCVTDQU` does it for 64 bits, here 32): a value that truncates below zero (`<= -1.0`) gives **0**, a **NaN or a value of `2^32` or more gives `0xffffffff`**, `-0.5` and `-0.0` give 0. No flag is set (the scalar instructions set the invalid flag; the lane operations never touch `FPSR`). |

**Naming and forms.** `vcvt.<from>.<to>`, the order of every other pair in the family (`vld.bu.w`, `vst.w.b`, `vacc.<src>.<acc>`, and the scalar `FCVTSL`, binary32 to signed long): `vcvt.l.f` is int32 to float and `vcvt.f.l` is float to int32. (The survey wrote the pair `vcvt.f.l`, `vcvt.l.f` with the explanation "int32 to float, float to int32" in that order; the set of names is the same, the meaning of each is this one.) The unsigned forms were added to the survey's two (the owner's list for wave 2 names signed and unsigned: LLVM's `uitofp` and `fptoui` are as common as the signed ones in quantisation code); there are no `d` (double) forms, which the survey does not list. 32-bit lanes only: 8 lanes in the simulator's 32-byte strip, a 16-bit or 64-bit lane to or from float goes through the load and store (`vld.w.l`, `vsts`).

**Float to integer truncates, as the scalar instruction, not "round to nearest even" as the survey's wording says.** The survey (5.1 item 11) wrote "float to int32 with round to nearest even"; the scalar `FCVTSL` and `FCVTDQ` truncate toward zero ("the rounding mode is in the opcode, never in a mode bit": `FRINT` rounds to nearest, `FRINTZ` truncates, and the integer conversions are the truncating ones), and a C cast and LLVM's `fptosi` are truncations. The lane agrees with the scalar word, so a vector loop and its scalar remainder cannot differ. A loop that wants rounding (`quantize_row_q8_0` uses `roundf`, ties away from zero) needs a vector rounding word (`vrint`, not in the family) before the conversion; this is a known gap, not an accident.

**What the assembler checks** (`vec_typecheck`): `vceq vcgt vcgtu` read both vectors as lanes of the suffix and write **integer** lanes of that size (`vcgt.f` reads 4-byte floating point, writes 4-byte integer); `vcvt.l.f` and `vcvt.lu.f` read 4-byte integer lanes and write 4-byte floating-point lanes, `vcvt.f.l` and `vcvt.f.lu` the reverse (so a `vcvt.f.l` result stored with `vst.f`, summed with `vacc.f`, or converted again by `vcvt.f.l`, is refused); `vbsl` refuses a mask and operands of different lane sizes (`vcgt.b` mask with halfword operands), and its result takes the lane type of the operand `vA` (or of `vB` when `vA` has none yet). Signedness is not checked, as everywhere else.

**What it does to the kernels.** No hand-written kernel of `arch-data/vec-forms/` had a compare idiom to shorten (the clamps are `vmin`/`vmax`, which cover them in one instruction, and `k_blend` is arithmetic): the survey said "replaces nothing in the compiled kernels". New kernels in `games/` show the forms that wave 2 makes possible, with the wave-1-only form beside each (`.vecw1.s`): `k_thresh` (`x > t ? a : b` on 16-bit values) 9 to 6 instructions a strip and no overflow limit (the wave-1 mask is `(t - x) >> 15`, right only while `t - x` fits 16 bits); `k_maskblend` (`m ? a : b` with a byte mask) 7 to 5; `k_quant` (`clamp(trunc(x * id), -128, 127)`, the activation quantisation, truncating) 4 instructions for 8 floats where wave 1 has only a scalar loop (about 9 instructions an element). Counts in errata 171.

**Not decided here.** Predication proper (a mask on the lane operations, masked loads and stores: an LLVM masked store needs `vbsl` with a read-modify-write of memory, a fault rule for the masked lanes, and is not in this wave); the less-than and not-equal compares (swap the operands of `vcgt`, or the arms of `vbsl`; for a float with a scalar operand and a NaN the swap of the arms differs from a true `<`, so a `vmov.f` of the scalar to a vector and the vector form is the exact way); a vector rounding word.

## 8. The strip is invisible (errata 173, 174, 175)

**The rule.** The strip of a block (the bytes of a vector register: `sim64 -S 16|32|64`, default 32) is the hardware's choice. One binary must give **bit-identical results, and the same sequence of faults with the same EPC, EVA and cause,** on an implementation of 128 bits and one of 256 bits; the program never names the width and the hardware never tells it. The owner's wording: "the program never names the width" (R471) and, on the strip study that listed the places where it could be seen: "yes do 2, close the 5 places" (R477). The study (`vec-strip.md` 5.1, `tools/strip-equiv.py`) found five; closing them showed a sixth (the pointer of `vfmab`) and a seventh that cannot be closed by a definition (overlapping streams, 8.8). This section is the architecture's statement; the semantics it changes are also written where they belong (sections 1, 2, 4, 6).

**The least strip is 16 bytes (128 bits)**, and every implementation's strip is a power of two from 16 bytes up to an implementation maximum (the simulator allows 16, 32 and 64). The reason is not taste: a strip of 8 bytes cannot hold a segment of four 4-byte fields in one beat, cannot hold the 16 entries of the table of `vtbl.b`, and cannot hold the 16-byte chunk that fixes the order of a floating-point sum (8.5). `sim64 -S 8` is **outside the architecture**: it still runs, for the cycle study of `vec-strip.md`, with a warning on standard error, and a program that sums floating-point lanes may give another result there.

### 8.1 The five places and what was done

| # | where a program (or the OS) could see the strip | decided | what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `vlen.b Rd` returned the bytes of a strip | **removed** (the word, its form, its tests, its manual entry); the one kernel that used it (`k_sgemm_nt.ukr.s`) is rewritten with a fixed tile of 4 columns, 8.4 | 1 word (363 words stay: `vfin` is added), 0 gates |
| 2 | the register form of `vtbl.b vD, vT, vI` took its table from the lanes of `vT`, so a short strip truncated it | **removed**; only `vtbl.b vD, (Ra), vI` remains (exactly 16 bytes at `(Ra)`, whatever the strip) | 0 gates (the same 16-way multiplexer; `vtbl_one` 12,663 against `vtbl_g16` 12,736 NAND2 in `vec-silicon.md`) |
| 3 | a `vclr` accumulator read inside the block (kernels 48, 49, 51 of `vec-restart`) showed the lanes of the strip | a block that names an accumulator **has a strip of exactly 16 bytes on every implementation**, so the lanes of an accumulator, and what a `vst` or `vacc` of it in the block gives, are the program's; the one way to read an accumulator out once, after the last strip, is the epilogue after the new `vfin`, 8.4 | one control rule (the strip of a block with accumulators is 16 bytes), `vfin`; wide implementations run an accumulator block at the width of 16 bytes |

### 8.2 `vlen` and the register form of `vtbl.b`: what changed

`vlen.b` is gone from the family (`vecfam.py`, the grammar, `as64`, the executor, the manual): the program cannot ask for `VL`. A program that needs a width for register blocking names an **architectural tile** (the micro-kernel: 8 rows by 4 columns, 4 columns being 16 bytes of binary32). `vtbl.b` has the memory form only: the table of a lookup is a 16-byte object in memory, so the lookup is the same on every implementation; a table that is in a vector register is stored first (`vst`) and looked up from memory. **`as64` refuses both** (the ordinary parse error for a form the grammar does not have: `tests/asm-err/removed-vlen-b.s`, `removed-vtbl-reg.s`).

### 8.3 What the strip may be

A block's `VL`, the elements of a strip, is the strip over the widest lane the block names, as before (`VBYTES / widest lane`), the last strip short. With the least strip of 16 bytes, `VL` is at least 16 / 8 = 2 (a block with a 64-bit lane) and the strips of every block are made of whole **16-byte groups** of any lane type except the last. The rules below that need it (the dot products' pairs and quadruples, the segment beats, the table, the chunks of a floating-point sum) hold because of this.

### 8.4 Accumulators, `vfin` and the micro-kernel

* A block **names accumulators** if it holds a `vclr`, or `vclr` named registers before it, or it holds a `vfin` or a `vfmab`. **The strip of such a block is 16 bytes on every implementation**: `VL = 16 / the widest lane`, whatever the hardware's strip. The lanes of an accumulator are then the program's: lane `i` of an accumulator of 4-byte lanes holds the sum of the elements of the stream whose index is `i` modulo 4. Every use of an accumulator inside the block is architectural: it may be the destination of `vmla`, `vfma`, `vfnma`, `vfmab`, `vadd`, may be stored by `vst` in every strip (the running sums by lane) or reduced by `vacc` in every strip (kernels 48, 49 and 51 of `vec-restart` keep their meaning). There is nothing to refuse, so `vec_typecheck` refuses nothing more here. (The assistant's first recommendation was to refuse every plain read of an accumulator and give one extraction form; the fixed strip makes each read well defined at no cost, and it also closes the floating-point accumulation of `vfma` and `vfmab` into accumulators, which the refusal alone would not.)
* **`vfin`** splits the instructions of the block: those before it run for every strip, those after it (the **epilogue**) run **once, after the last strip**, as one more strip of `VL` lanes (`VL` as above) with no stream: a `(Ra)+` operand moves once, a `d(Ra)` operand does not move, a load loads `VL` elements, and the accumulators hold their final values. A `vacc`, `vmaxacc` or `vst` of an accumulator in the epilogue is the strip-independent way to get an accumulator out; a `vld` and a `vadd` in the epilogue can add the old content of a C tile to it (the micro-kernel does this). The epilogue is one strip for faults: its accesses are checked before any of it runs (8.6), its stores are made together, and a fault in it restarts the whole block from its entry (hidden state, 8.6). `vfin` is allowed once in a block; with no accumulator named by anything else it still makes the block an accumulator block (the strip of 16 bytes).
* **The micro-kernel** (`docs/encoding-work/arch-data/vec-forms/blas/k_sgemm_nt.ukr.s`, run by `tests/sim/vec-strip.s` at every strip): 8 rows by 4 columns of C in eight accumulators, one strip per k step (B packed 4 floats for each k, A packed 8 for each k), `vfmab.f v0..v7, v8, (r0)+`, then after `vfin` eight times `vld.f v9,(Rc); vadd.f v9,v9,vN; vst.f v9,(Rc)` with the eight row pointers made before the block. 49 instructions against the old kernel's 51: 11 to set up the end of the panel and the eight row pointers, the block of 37 (10 for each strip, `vfin`, 24 in the epilogue), `ret`; the old one spent 5 on `vlen`, the shift and the multiply for the end of the panel and 8 scalar `add`s between the rows, which the pointers replace.
* **What it costs.** One control rule (the width of a block that has accumulators or a `vfmab`: 16 bytes) and the epilogue (run the instructions after `vfin` once at the end). The accumulator blocks of a 256-bit implementation run at half its width. They are ranked last (no compiled kernel makes one: `llvm/vector-usage.md`), and the gain of fixing them is that the hidden state is small, defined and the same everywhere. A wide implementation may of course keep more accumulator rows in flight for different blocks; it may not change what one block computes.

### 8.5 The floating-point sum: the canonical order (errata 174)

`vacc.f`, `vacc.f.d` and `vacc.d` add the lanes of the stream to a scalar register in this order, whatever the strip:

1. the lanes of the stream are cut into **chunks of 16 bytes of source lanes**, in element order: 4 binary32 lanes or 2 binary64 lanes (a short last chunk has the lanes it has: 1, 2 or 3);
2. each chunk is summed by the fixed tree `(l0 + l1) + (l2 + l3)` (`l0 + l1` for two lanes, `(l0 + l1) + l2` for three) in the **accumulate precision** (`vacc.f`: binary32, `vacc.f.d` and `vacc.d`: binary64; the lanes of `vacc.f.d` are converted exactly), every addition rounded to nearest even;
3. the chunks are added **to the register one after the other**: `R = ((R + c0) + c1) + c2 ...`.

A strip is a whole number of chunks except the last, so the strip does not change the order; the register is committed with the strip as every reduction is, so **a fault restarts at the strip with nothing hidden**. The assembler enforces the one condition: **a floating-point sum may not be in a block that has a lane wider than its source lane** (a `vld.q` beside a `vacc.f`: the strip would not be whole chunks; `tests/asm-err/fsum-wide-lane.s`, `fsum-fd-lane.s`; the LLVM pass makes no such block). `vmaxacc` and `vminacc` take the lanes in element order (a comparison is exact; only the sign of a zero and a NaN could show an order, and it is the stream's). The floating-point lane operations (`vfma`, `vfir`, ...) are lane by lane and never depended on the strip; the accumulation of `vfma` and `vfmab` into accumulators is the accumulator rule of 8.4. Integer sums are exact and were never affected (`vacc`, `vdacc`, `vsad`).

**The options, with their pros and cons, and the measurement** (the silicon is `tools/gates/vecred.v`, `run-red.sh`, `strip-results/red.tsv`: NAND2 equivalents as `strip-synth.sh` counts them, flip-flops at 6, a floating-point add `fadd32` 2,331; one run is one strip width; the old strip-dependent tree, the reference, is the same as the chosen one):

### 8.6 Faults: what is exact, what is opaque (errata 173)

* **EPC** is the address of the `vec` instruction (a block is one instruction for the purpose of restart: 4.4), as before. **ECAUSE** and **EVA** are those of **the first faulting access in the order of the stream's elements**: the access with the least element index, the first instruction of the block among accesses of the same element (and the first field of a segment), where the table of `vtbl`, the scalar of `vfmab` and the epilogue belong to the strip's first element and the neighbours that `vfir` reads beyond a strip to their own element. So EVA is the same at every strip. The hardware checks all the accesses of a strip (alignment, mapping, permission) before it does any of them, in this order, so a strip that faults has done nothing: no store, no register changed (the abort is precise and the strip is annulled whole).
* **The pointer registers and the scalar result** (the `(Ra)+` pointers, `Rd` of a reduction) that a handler reads are **at an element boundary chosen by the implementation, no later than the faulting element** (the strip's start in this implementation; the entry of the block for a block with accumulators or a `vfin`, which restarts from its entry). **The operating system treats them as opaque: it restarts by returning, never by interpreting or adjusting them** (adding to a pointer the part of a strip that it thinks was done would be wrong at another width). A program's own handler (a signal handler) may not rely on them either.
* **The memory a handler sees**: the stores of the elements before the progress element are in memory; the stores from the faulting element on are not; between them it is the implementation's (the strips it had committed, or the ones a block that restarts from its entry will repeat).
* **Hidden state restarts clean**: an accumulator block (8.4) restarts from its entry, the hardware keeping a hidden copy of the naming of its accumulators made at the `vec`; the accumulators themselves are discarded; the work repeated is at most the block so far, bounded if the program bounds the block. A block with only floating-point sums (8.5) and the other reductions keeps nothing and restarts at the strip.
* **`tests/sim/vec-restart.S`** (generated by `gen_vec_restart.py`, 867 rows, 53 kernels) checks this at every strip: a fault in a load, a store or a table, in the first, a middle and the last strip, in a block of one element, with a misaligned pointer, **with two streams faulting in one block** (the first EVA must be the stream with the least element), and **with a handler that reads EVA, EPC and ECAUSE only** (clob 3) and returns: the result of the restarted block must equal the run with no fault. The state a full handler saw is checked as a relation: every pointer is its entry value plus the same number of elements `e'`, `e'` no later than the faulting element (0 for a block with accumulators), the scalar equals the model's after exactly `e'` elements, and the memory holds the model before `e'`, the initial bytes from the faulting element on, and either between: the same program at every strip.

### 8.7 What the test suite proves (and how it was shown not to be empty)

`tools/check-sim64.sh` runs `vec-basic`, `vec-wave1`, `vec-wave2`, `vec-restart` and the new `vec-strip` at the default strip and again at 16, 32 and 64 (the `NAME.strips` files), each unmodified; `tools/strip-equiv.py` runs the whole `tests/sim` suite and `llvm/check.sh` at 16, 32 and 64 with no check switched off, and the probe (a sum of 1000 values of adversarial size by `vacc.f`, `vacc.d` and `vacc.f.d` must give the bits of a model of the canonical order at every strip). `tests/sim/vec-strip.s` (generated by `gen_vec_strip.py`, 23 checks, expected values from a Python model written from this section and not from a run) holds the sums of 1000 values and of 999 (a short last chunk), the maximum and minimum with signed zeros, the integer reductions, `vfma`, `vfir` with its halo, segments, a strided load, `vtbl`, compare, select and conversion, the narrowing stores, the running accumulator read in the block, a `vfin` epilogue and the micro-kernel. Non-empty: a copy of the executor with the sum taken over the whole strip fails it at strips 32 and 64 (check 1) and passes at 16, where the strip is one chunk; a copy with the accesses checked in the order of the strip instead of the elements fails `vec-restart` at strips 32 and 64 (check 3, the first EVA).

### 8.8 What a program or the OS may still see

* **Time.** Cycle counts, and anything made of them, differ with the width: that is what a wider strip is for. (Timing side channels are the business of the constant-time rules of the security chapters, not of this section.)
* **Overlapping streams.** If the store stream of a block overlaps a load stream (or another store stream) at an element other than the same one, the result depended on the strip (the loads of a strip are done before its stores, so a load that reads what an earlier lane of the same strip stores sees the old value, and what a strip stores, the next strip loads). **Unpredictable: the program is wrong** (as `memcpy` with overlapping buffers); the LLVM pass keeps the scalar loop unless it proves the streams disjoint or the same element (`vec-isa.md` 5, `llvm/README.md`). The old text (4.5: "the result of overlapping streams in different strips is the sequential one") is withdrawn. A fault of the hardware cannot do this to a correct program.
* **The memory a handler sees** between the progress element and the faulting element (8.6), and the pointers and the scalar result there, which the OS must not use.
* **The strip of 8 bytes**, outside the architecture.
* Nothing else: the exhaustive list of what `tools/strip-equiv.py` compares is every program of `tests/sim` (exit status and output) and the 88 vector programs and 61 `.ll` tests of `llvm/check.sh`, at 16, 32 and 64.

## 9. The 2D (tile) block (errata 176; the design, the options, the measurements and the prior art are in `vec-2d.md`)

`vec2 Rs, Re, Rn, Rw`, then up to four `vskip Ra, Rg`, then the body of a 1D block, then `vend`: the block runs for `Rn` rows, each the region `[Rs, Re)` of the first stream (`Rw` bytes of it), its strips as in 1D (a strip never spans rows; the last strip of a row is short), and at the end of each row every `vskip` pointer `Ra` moves by its gap register `Rg` (the stream's pitch minus the bytes its row used), `Re` moves by `Rw` plus the gap of `Rs`, and `Rn` falls by one.

* **State.** All of it is in ordinary integer registers: `Rs` and the other pointers (progress within the row), `Re` (the end of the current row), `Rn` (rows not yet completed, the current one included), `Rw` (the constant width) and the gaps. Nothing is hidden; a context switch saves nothing extra.
* **Commit.** The row advance is committed with the last strip of its row (one atomic step with the strip's pointers and reductions). A fault in a strip annuls the strip, `EPC` is the `vec2`, `EVA` and `ECAUSE` are those of the first faulting access in the order of the elements (rows in order), and the same `vec2` executed again continues at that strip of that row; the pointers, `Re` and `Rn` that a handler reads are opaque (as 8.6).
* **Results.** A reduction register accumulates over the whole tile. A pointer with a `vskip` ends at its entry value plus rows x pitch; one without is contiguous (packed tiles need no declaration); a `d(Ra)` stream moves only if it has a `vskip`. The strip is invisible (section 8 holds: tests/sim/vec-2d.S at strips 16, 32, 64).
* **Rules.** `Rs`, `Re`, `Rn`, `Rw` are four different registers; `Rw`, the gaps, `Re`, `Rn`, the skipped pointers and the targets of reductions are told apart (what the block writes and what it only reads): a block that breaks this is illegal; `Re - Rs` and `Rw` are whole numbers of elements of the first stream; `Rn` or `Rw` zero does nothing; no accumulators (`vclr`, `vfmab`, `vfin` are refused in a 2D block); no one-instruction prefix form; nested blocks are refused; overlapping streams are unpredictable as in 1D.
* **Cost in the models.** `sim64 --vrow=N` (default 1 cycle for each row advance, an assumption; 0 and 2 are in the tables of `vec-2d.md`); each `vskip` and the `vend` take one decode slot once per block.
* **Compiler.** Not emitted; the name tables of LLVM know the words.
