## 1. Diagnosis (grammars of enc-hb on the new compiler's code; docs/encoding-proposal.md 12.1)
* Sites: /16 34,387 cbCC (1-parcel 7,839; 2-parcel 26,220), /32 34,524 (6,269; 27,389). Substituted by cmp+bCC: **328 (0.95%) and 866 (2.51%)**.
* Cause: wide immediate (> 12 bits; widest cbCC immediate was 12 bits in /32, 16 bits for cbeq/cbne only in /16): 284/328 and 682/866. Only 44 and 184 have an immediate some form holds but a pc field too short. It is NOT distance: 58% of the 866 (29% of the 328) are within 256 bytes. Worst projects: mbedtls 220 / 583 (library error-code constants), zlib 54 / 116, lua 11 / 74.
* Mechanism: code space. cbCC leaves took 19,314 first parcels (29.5%) in /16 but 6,144 (9.4%) in /32 (2-parcel immediate forms 15,842 vs 3,496), same 95% budget: one more register bit halves the (imm, pc) combinations a share of the space buys, and in /32 each combination exists hot (4-bit) and general (5-bit); loads/stores/moves won the space.
* Dynamic: executed substituted pairs 3,958,721 (0.61% of 647.1 M) in /16, 6,233,099 (1.02% of 611.4 M) in /32; Lua 3.96 M / 6.13 M, from luaV_execute's binary-search dispatch (cbhi r0,#41,far; one site 2.05 M times). The 38.8 M of the phase-2 report belonged to the old compiler's compare chain (gone).
* Optimiser flaw found: solve.py credited every cbCC form with (99 - own size) bytes per site ("unencodable" baseline) although the real alternative is cmp+bCC of the same bytes; and the distances fitted were those of the ORIGINAL encoding's lengths.

## 4. Verdict
After re-optimisation pdp64/32 is STILL worse than pdp64/16 in fetched bytes (+3.27%, was +1.40%) and code size (+3.32% corpus text, +3.92% linked text of the six programs), for -5.90% executed instructions and -63.5% frame accesses. The cbCC problem is solved (pairs 1.02% -> 0.013% of executed instructions in /32; 0.61% -> 0.00% in /16), which is why the gap WIDENED: the fix paid /16 (-2.12% fetched bytes) far more than /32 (-0.32%). The cost is the 5-bit register field: the same 16-register code in the /32 encoding fetches +10.75% (static +4.23%); the 32-register compile then fetches 6.76% less than that (about two thirds repaid). Crypto kernels pay most (bs_sym +14.7%, bs_ec +16.5%). It does not change the section-10 verdict (32 registers not worth it for size/fetch alone); what it changes is that the instruction-count and frame-traffic advantage is now measured without the cbCC artefact.
