# Sources opened for the question "why does almost no shipping architecture keep sequential consistency, and what does it cost"

Every file below was downloaded on 2026-11-25 into a scratch directory outside the repository (PDFs are not kept in the repository), converted with `pdftotext` (or, for a scanned or font-damaged PDF, rendered to images and read page by page), and read. "Read" says which parts. Quotes are under 25 words and exact. "Address" is where the file was fetched; hosts that refuse plain downloads (Rice repository, UCL) were fetched through a headless Firefox as `tools/browse.py` does.

Nothing here is quoted from a file that was not opened. What I did not open is listed at the end.

## Not opened, so not used in the conclusions

* Cain and Lipasti's replay filters beyond their abstract and section 3 (the performance tables of section 5 were read; the circuit sections were not).
* "Understanding Real-World Ordering-Related Concurrency Bugs under Weak-Memory and Cross-Architecture Settings" (an ACM page of 2026 found by search, 58 bugs): the ACM site refused a plain download and I did not obtain the file, so I only know its abstract from a search result and I do not cite its numbers.
* The Intel optimisation manual and the AMD manual (for the AMD ordering text); the Arm Architecture Reference Manual (I used the A64 instruction pages instead); the Power ISA of version 3.0 and 3.1 (I read version 2.02 of 2005, the last I could download); the R10000 manual version 2.0 and the R12000 to R16000 manuals (blocked or not found); the SGI statement of why the Origin line moved off MIPS.
* Speculative sequential consistency papers other than those above (Gniady's PACT 2002 paper, Lin, Nagarajan and Gupta ASPLOS 2012): found by search, not opened.
