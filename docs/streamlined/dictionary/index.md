# The instruction dictionary

[← manual index](../00-index.md)

This is the reference part of the manual: **one entry for each instruction**, in the layout of a processor reference manual. Each entry gives its mnemonics, its syntax, its encoding (the parcel diagram and the numbers), its operation in pseudocode, the flags it writes, the exceptions it can take, and where the book explains it. The entries are generated from `spec/pdp64-mnemonics.hbnf`, the one place where the numbers of the architecture are written, so an encoding here cannot disagree with the assembler or the simulators; the words (operation, flags, exceptions) are in `tools/manual/isa_entries.py` and were checked against the reference executor, `common/pdp64exec.c`, which `sim64` and QEMU share. Where the executor does not implement an instruction yet, its entry says so.

**Notation.** `Rd` is the destination register (the `reg` field of parcel 0), `Rs` the first source (`low`), `Rt` the second (`t` of parcel 1), `Ru` the third (`x`); the assembler writes the destination last. `rm` is a general operand: a register at `len = 00`, or a memory operand, an immediate or an absolute address by one of the eight addressing modes (book section 9.5). `EA(rm)` is its effective address. `M[a]` is memory, `M8[a]` one byte. `sext` and `zext` sign- and zero-extend. A `len = 01` instruction is two parcels (four bytes) and a `len = 10` instruction three (six bytes); `len = 11` is page 2. `N Z V C` are the condition flags. Bit diagrams number the bits of a parcel from 15 at the left.

**Cf.** lines point to the chapter that explains the matter an instruction touches: the kernel (chapter 6), the multiprocessor model (chapter 7), the bus (chapter 2).
